Graduation Rate

Graduation Rate was selected because I believe it is one of the strongest indicators of a college’s ability to support students academically and socially throughout their education. A higher graduation rate suggests that students are more likely to remain enrolled, receive adequate support, and successfully complete their degrees. I considered this category especially important because attending college is a long-term investment, and graduation outcomes provide a measurable way to evaluate whether students are actually reaching the end goal of earning a degree.

Statistical Summary: Graduation Rate

The average graduation rate for the colleges in this dataset is about 84%. This means most of the schools have a pretty solid rate of students actually finishing their degrees.

The standard deviation is around 0.10 (about 10%), which shows there is some difference between schools, but most of them are still pretty close to the average. A few schools like Duke and Dartmouth are higher than the rest, while a few are lower.

Graduation Rate: Z-Score & Percentile

For University of Virginia, the graduation rate is 94%. The z-score for this value is about +1.0, meaning it is about one standard deviation above the average graduation rate.

This corresponds to roughly the 84th percentile, so UVA performs better than most colleges in this dataset when it comes to graduation rate.
