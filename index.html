Project Overview

For my College Rankings Project, I designed a weighted system to evaluate five major colleges using a combination of academic performance metrics and real-world student decision factors. The goal of this project was not to simply rank colleges based on prestige, but to create a more realistic model of how students actually choose schools in practice.

I selected five categories: Graduation Rate, Annual Cost, Public Transportation Accessibility, Student Population, and Distance From Home. These categories were chosen because they represent both measurable academic outcomes and personal factors that influence college decisions. Each category was assigned a weight based on how important I believed it should be in determining an overall ranking.

How I Weighted Each Category

For my ranking system, I assigned different weights to each category based on how important I personally thought they were when choosing a college. I wanted the system to reflect real student decision-making instead of just academic reputation.

Graduation Rate (30%) — This was the most important category because it shows whether students actually finish their degree. If a school has a high graduation rate, it usually means students are supported well academically.

Annual Cost (30%) — I weighted this equally with graduation rate because affordability is a huge factor. Even a great school isn’t realistic if it’s too expensive.

Public Transportation (20%) — This mattered because transportation affects daily life, internships, and access to opportunities outside campus.

Student Population (10%) — School size affects experience, but I didn’t think it was as important as cost or outcomes.

Distance From Home (10%) — This was the least important factor since students can choose to go far or stay close depending on preference.

Overall, I prioritized long-term outcomes (graduation) and affordability over convenience-based factors like distance or school size.

Top 5 Ranked Colleges

#1 University of Virginia

Strongest Areas: Graduation Rate, Cost

Weakest Area: Distance

Virginia came out on top mostly because it just felt the most balanced overall. The graduation rate is really strong, which made it stand out right away, and the cost isn’t too extreme compared to other top schools. Since those two categories mattered the most in my system, it naturally ended up #1. The only downside is that it’s kind of far from Cambridge, but that didn’t hurt it enough to change its ranking.

#2 University of Georgia

Strongest Areas: Cost, Student Population

Weakest Area: Distance

Georgia ranked really high because it’s affordable and has a huge student population, which makes it feel like there are a lot of opportunities and things going on. It’s the kind of school that feels accessible for a lot of students. The only thing that really pulled it down was how far away it is, but overall it still scored really well.

#3 Washington University in St. Louis

Strongest Areas: Graduation Rate

Weakest Areas: Cost, Distance

WashU is one of those schools that does really well academically, especially with graduation rate. That’s basically what kept it so high in my ranking. The downside is that it’s expensive and far away, which hurt it in a couple categories. But even with that, the academic strength carried it pretty far.

#4 Duke University

Strongest Areas: Graduation Rate

Weakest Area: Cost

Duke is kind of similar — it does really well academically, especially with graduation rate, so it automatically scored high in my system. The main issue is just the cost, since it’s one of the more expensive schools. Even though that brought it down a bit, the academic side still pushed it into the top 5.

#5 Dartmouth College

Strongest Areas: Graduation Rate, Distance

Weakest Area: Student Population

Dartmouth made it into the top 5 because it has strong academics and is also relatively closer compared to a lot of the other schools I looked at. The smaller student population was a downside since it means fewer resources and opportunities compared to bigger schools. But overall, it still felt strong enough to stay in the top group.
